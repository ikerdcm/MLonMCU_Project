Keyword Spotting on Edge MCUs

A cross-platform study: same task, four accelerators, two optimization ladders

ETH Zürich · Machine Learning on Microcontrollers · 2026

Pascal — AI85KWS (KWS20)  ·  Iker — DS-CNN-L

One-line pitch: we take always-on keyword spotting and ask (A) which MCU runs it best, and (B) how far we can push one model with optimization. Two model families, four boards.

The application — always-on Keyword Spotting

  • Task: detect spoken keywords from a 1 s mic window, on-device.
  • Why on the MCU? always-on, <mW budget, low latency, privacy (no audio leaves the chip).
  • Pipeline: 16 kHz audio → MFCC features → CNN → class.
  • Dataset: Google Speech Commands.
StageDetail
Audio window1.0–1.024 s @ 16 kHz
FeaturesMFCC (e.g. 49×10)
Modelsmall CNN (DS-CNN / ai8x)
Classes12 (DS-CNN) / 20-kw (AI85KWS)

Inference is tiny vs the 1 s window → energy/inference, not user latency, is the real edge metric.

KWS is the canonical TinyML task — "Hey Google" style. The point of running it on an MCU is power and privacy, not raw speed.

Two questions drive the study

Story A — Which MCU wins?

Take the same model, deploy across boards, compare latency · energy · memory · accuracy at a matched (int8) config.

Story B — How far can we push one?

Take the champion and climb an optimization ladder: fp32 → int8 → prune → distill. Where's the ceiling, and what bounds it?

Spectrum: dedicated TPU (Coral) → in-SoC CNN accelerator (MAX) → CPU + DSP kernels (U5) → multi-core RISC-V + NPU (GAP9).

The boards span the full design space. Crucially only MAX and GAP9 do native sub-8-bit — that gates the 4-bit story later.

Methodology — one disciplined process

  • Versioning: every optimization is a new version — never overwrite, so before/after is always measurable.
  • Two modes: offline (latency on a fixed vector) + online (live mic, power CSV).
  • Metrics: CNN latency, energy/inference, flash (.text), SRAM, accuracy.
  • Single source of truth: a results ledger every measurement feeds into.

Part 1 — AI85KWS

KWS20-v3 · STM32U5 vs MAX78000 · completed

Now the finished half of the study: the 20-keyword AI85KWS model, taken across U5 and MAX with quantization and pruning.

AI85KWS — the KWS20-v3 model

  • Task: 20 keywords + unknown/silence → 21 classes (harder than 12-class).
  • Arch: ai8x CNN, 8 conv layers, MaxPool, final Gemm.
  • Compute: ~8.3 M ops (baseline) → ~7.2 M (pruned "w90").
  • Deployed on: MAX78000 (CNN accelerator) and STM32U5 (CMSIS-NN).

Two optimization axes explored:

  • Quantization — fp32 → int8 (QAT, true-2D NCHW → CubeAI for U5).
  • Pruning — the w90 variant (structured width reduction).

Accuracy numbers from the ai8x / U5 eval — to fill from Pascal's results.

KWS20 is a 20-keyword task — broader and harder than the 12-class DS-CNN. w90 is the pruned variant used on both boards.

Takeaways:

  • A dedicated CNN accelerator wins decisively — ~92× on the identical model.
  • Quantization is the big lever on the CPU (2.3×); pruning is secondary (~10% / ~8%) on both.
  • But remember: end-to-end is still dominated by the 1 s audio window on both boards.
This is Story A for the AI85KWS model: the accelerator is ~92× faster on the exact same network. Pruning is a minor refinement next to quantization + the accelerator.
